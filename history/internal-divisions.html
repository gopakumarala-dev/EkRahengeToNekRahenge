UNITY AND
DIVISION

The history of Bharat includes long periods of political fragmentation as well as periods of larger political consolidation.

Kingdoms and ruling houses often competed for territory, resources, influence and political authority. Alliances could change as circumstances changed.

QUESTION 01

WHO WERE
THE RIVALS?

Rivalries could involve neighbouring kingdoms, competing dynasties, succession claimants, regional powers or larger imperial formations.

QUESTION 02

WHAT CAUSED
THE CONFLICT?

Territory, succession, resources, political authority, strategic interests and changing alliances could contribute to conflict.

QUESTION 03

DID ALLIANCES
CHANGE?

Political alliances were often shaped by circumstances. A ruler or kingdom could become an ally at one time and an opponent at another.

QUESTION 04

WHAT WAS
THE OUTCOME?

Internal conflicts could result in territorial changes, new ruling houses, political consolidation, fragmentation or the emergence of new powers.

POLITICAL UNITY WAS NOT STATIC.

Political boundaries changed repeatedly across centuries. Periods of fragmentation could be followed by periods of consolidation, while regional powers continued to play important roles.
