A HISTORICAL QUESTION

WHAT DO THE
SOURCES RECORD?

Captivity and slavery appear in historical records from different periods and regions. The circumstances, scale and nature of captivity could vary considerably.

Historical investigation should therefore distinguish between different forms of captivity, forced labour, enslavement, deportation and the treatment of prisoners.

QUESTION 01

WHO WAS
CAPTURED?

Sources may describe prisoners of war, civilians, soldiers, political opponents or other groups. Each account must be examined in its own context.

QUESTION 02

WHAT HAPPENED
TO THEM?

Historical records may describe imprisonment, forced labour, enslavement, ransom, deportation, release or other outcomes.

QUESTION 03

WHEN DID
IT HAPPEN?

Dates and chronology should be established from the strongest available evidence and should distinguish contemporary accounts from later narratives.

QUESTION 04

WHAT WAS
THE CONTEXT?

Warfare, political conflict, economic interests, social structures and other historical circumstances may all be relevant to understanding individual events.

BEHIND EVERY RECORD WAS A HUMAN LIFE.

Historical records often reduce complex human experiences to brief descriptions. Where the evidence permits, the experiences of individuals and communities should also be examined.
