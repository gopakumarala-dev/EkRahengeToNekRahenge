COLONIAL PERIOD

A PERIOD OF
MAJOR TRANSFORMATION

European trading companies established commercial and political influence in different parts of the Indian subcontinent before the expansion of British territorial rule.

Colonial rule brought major changes in administration, taxation, trade, law, education, infrastructure and political institutions. These developments need to be examined through contemporary records and historical scholarship.

POLITICAL EXPANSION

FROM TRADING
POWER TO TERRITORIAL RULE

European commercial interests developed into political and military influence in several regions. The British East India Company became a major territorial power before the transfer of authority to the British Crown in 1858.

The expansion of colonial authority occurred through warfare, diplomacy, treaties, alliances, annexations and administrative restructuring.

LAND, REVENUE

Colonial economic policy affected land revenue systems, agricultural production, taxation, trade and patterns of commerce.

The economic history of the colonial period requires examination of revenue records, trade statistics, official reports, company records and other contemporary evidence.

EDUCATION & INSTITUTIONS

NEW INSTITUTIONS
& NEW DEBATES

Colonial rule transformed systems of formal education and established institutions that influenced administration, professional life and intellectual activity.

The history of education during this period includes both institutional changes and debates concerning language, curriculum, access and indigenous systems of learning.

RESISTANCE TO COLONIAL RULE

Opposition to colonial authority took many forms, including local uprisings, military resistance, political movements, social mobilisation and organised campaigns.

1857

THE GREAT
UPRISING

The uprising of 1857 was a major political and military challenge to East India Company rule. Its causes, course, participants and consequences remain important subjects of historical study.

After the uprising, the Government of India Act 1858 transferred the administration of British India from the East India Company to the British Crown.

THE ROAD TO INDEPENDENCE

Political organisations, social reform movements, intellectuals, revolutionaries and mass movements contributed in different ways to the struggle against colonial rule.

1947

INDEPENDENCE

British rule in India ended in 1947, leading to independence and the creation of new political arrangements in the subcontinent.

The transition involved profound political, social and human consequences that must be studied through contemporary documentation and historical scholarship.

STUDY THE RECORD.

Colonial history should be examined through official records, administrative reports, correspondence, legislation, newspapers, archaeological evidence, economic data, memoirs and other contemporary sources.
