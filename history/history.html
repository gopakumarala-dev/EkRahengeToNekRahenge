THE FIRST QUESTION

HOW DO YOU TELL
A HISTORY SPANNING
THOUSANDS OF YEARS?

Not as a list of dates. Not as a parade of rulers. And not as a story that begins with the creation of the modern Republic.

The story begins with the land and the people. It moves through prehistoric communities, early settlements, cities and kingdoms; through Vedic traditions and Itihāsa; through philosophy, mathematics, medicine, astronomy, literature and art; through temples, pilgrimage and trade; through encounters, conflicts, resistance and renewal; through colonial rule and the long struggle for freedom; and finally into independent India.

Throughout the journey we ask another question: What did people actually do, believe, build, preserve and pass to the next generation?

THE CHANGING MAP

FROM BHARATVARSHA
TO PRESENT-DAY INDIA

The political geography of the subcontinent changed repeatedly. Kingdoms expanded and contracted. Empires rose and disappeared. New political powers emerged. Colonial rule imposed new administrative structures. Partition created a new international frontier.

At the same time, civilisational memory could travel across political boundaries through sacred geography, pilgrimage, language, literature, trade and tradition.

THE LESSON OF HISTORY

GLORY WITH
THE COURAGE TO LEARN

We celebrate achievement because it deserves to be remembered. We examine failure because the next generation deserves to learn from it.

UNITY Diversity need not destroy a larger sense of civilisational belonging.
DIGNITY A society becomes stronger when social exclusion and discrimination are challenged.
PREPAREDNESS Political complacency and strategic mistakes can carry consequences across generations.
RENEWAL Preserving a civilisation and reforming it are not contradictory tasks.
