WHAT DID THE
ORIGINAL SOURCE SAY?

Start with the source itself whenever it is available. Identify its language, date, authorship, location and physical or textual form.

Then distinguish the words or material evidence itself from later descriptions or interpretations.

ARCHAEOLOGY

LET THE
MATERIAL RECORD SPEAK.

Archaeological evidence includes settlements, structures, objects, inscriptions, layers, artefacts and other physical remains.

Material evidence can provide information that complements written records and can also raise questions about traditional narratives.

EXPLORE THE ATLAS →

MANUSCRIPTS & LITERARY RECORDS

Literary and manuscript traditions preserve information about ideas, institutions, beliefs, social life, literature and intellectual history.

Their date, transmission history, authorship, purpose and later copying all need to be considered when using them as historical evidence.

CORROBORATION

ONE SOURCE
IS RARELY THE WHOLE STORY.

Compare different types of evidence. A written account can be considered alongside inscriptions, coins, architecture and archaeological findings.

Agreement between independent lines of evidence can strengthen a conclusion. Differences can reveal uncertainty or competing interpretations.
