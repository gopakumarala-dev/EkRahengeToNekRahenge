OUR SOURCES

FOLLOW THE EVIDENCE.

History is built from evidence. Our aim is to make important historical claims traceable to sources, context and scholarship.

01 • WHY SOURCES MATTER

DON'T JUST
TAKE OUR WORD.

A historical claim becomes more meaningful when the reader can examine the evidence behind it. Sources help us understand what happened, what people believed, what institutions recorded and what physical evidence survives.

No single type of source can answer every historical question. An inscription, an archaeological discovery, a contemporary account and a modern scholarly work each provide different kinds of information.

We therefore look at sources in context and, wherever possible, compare evidence from different sources.

OUR BASIC PRINCIPLE

Claim → Original Source → Context → Cross-check → Conclusion

02 • SOURCE CATEGORIES

MANY SOURCES.
ONE QUESTION.

Historical research draws upon different forms of evidence. Each has strengths, limitations and a particular historical context.

01

PRIMARY SOURCES

Documents, records and other material originating close to the period or event being studied.

02

ARCHAEOLOGY

Structures, artefacts, monuments, excavated remains and other physical evidence from the past.

03

INSCRIPTIONS

Epigraphic records on stone, metal and other materials that preserve information about people, institutions and events.

04

LITERARY SOURCES

Historical, religious, literary and other texts that preserve information and traditions from different periods.

05

FOREIGN ACCOUNTS

Observations and records produced by visitors, travellers, diplomats and writers from outside the region.

06

INSTITUTIONAL RECORDS

Government publications, official records, archaeological reports, archives and other institutional material.

07

SCHOLARSHIP

Academic books, research papers, critical editions and other works of historical scholarship.

08

ORAL TRADITIONS

Traditions, memories and narratives passed between generations, considered alongside other available evidence.

03 • HOW WE USE SOURCES

FROM CLAIM
TO CONCLUSION.

01

IDENTIFY THE CLAIM

What exactly is being claimed? A precise claim is easier to investigate than a broad statement.

02

FIND THE SOURCE

We look for the earliest or most relevant available evidence rather than relying only on repeated secondary claims.

03

CHECK THE CONTEXT

Date, authorship, purpose, language, audience and circumstances can affect how a source should be understood.

04

CROSS-CHECK

Where possible, evidence is compared with other independent sources and forms of evidence.

05

DISTINGUISH FACT FROM INTERPRETATION

A source may document an event without automatically proving every interpretation later attached to it.

06

ACKNOWLEDGE UNCERTAINTY

Where evidence is incomplete or disputed, that limitation should be clearly stated.

04 • READING EVIDENCE

NOT ALL SOURCES
ANSWER THE SAME QUESTION.

?

PROVENANCE

Who created the source? When was it created? Where did it originate?

?

PURPOSE

Why was it created? Who was its intended audience?

?

TRANSMISSION

Has the source survived in its original form, or has it been copied, translated or transmitted through later generations?

05 • TRANSPARENCY

SHOW THE
SOURCE.

The purpose of this project is not to ask readers to accept our conclusions without examination. Where a historical claim is important, we aim to make the evidence behind it visible and traceable.
