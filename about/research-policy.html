RESEARCH POLICY

FOLLOW THE EVIDENCE.

Our approach to history begins with evidence, continues with context and remains open to revision when better evidence becomes available.

01 • OUR CORE PRINCIPLE

NO BLIND
BELIEF.

Historical narratives can be shaped by the sources that survive, the people who recorded them and the interpretations that followed.

We therefore distinguish between what a source directly records, what can reasonably be inferred from it and what remains interpretation or debate.

OUR STANDARD

Evidence before assertion.
Context before conclusion.
Questions before certainty.

02 • OUR RESEARCH PROCESS

CLAIM →
EVIDENCE →
CONCLUSION.

01

DEFINE

Identify the historical claim or question precisely before attempting to answer it.

02

LOCATE

Find the relevant primary sources, physical evidence and established scholarship.

03

VERIFY

Examine provenance, chronology, authorship, transmission and reliability.

04

COMPARE

Compare relevant evidence and identify where sources agree, differ or remain incomplete.

03 • SOURCE CONTEXT

A SOURCE
HAS A STORY.

01

WHO?

Who created, recorded or transmitted the source?

02

WHEN?

When was it created, and how close was it to the event being described?

03

WHY?

What purpose did the source serve, and who was its intended audience?

04

HOW?

How has the evidence survived, been copied, translated, edited or interpreted?

04 • DISTINGUISHING EVIDENCE

FACT.
INTERPRETATION.
UNCERTAINTY.

01

DOCUMENTED

What the available evidence directly records or establishes.

02

INTERPRETED

Conclusions drawn from evidence that may involve scholarly interpretation.

03

UNCERTAIN

Questions where the surviving evidence is incomplete, conflicting or insufficient.

05 • SCHOLARLY DISAGREEMENT

HISTORY
CAN BE DEBATED.

Historical scholarship does not always produce a single interpretation. Different scholars may evaluate the same evidence differently.

Where significant interpretations differ, we should identify the disagreement rather than conceal it.

Readers should be able to distinguish between established evidence and competing explanations of that evidence.

PRINCIPLE

Disagreement is not a reason to hide the evidence.

06 • CORRECTIONS & REVISION

RESEARCH
CAN CHANGE.

Historical understanding is not permanently fixed. New discoveries, better translations, newly accessible archives and improved scholarship can change how the past is understood.

01

NEW EVIDENCE

Important new evidence may require earlier conclusions to be reconsidered.

02

CORRECTIONS

Errors identified in our material should be corrected rather than defended.

03

TRANSPARENCY

Where appropriate, significant changes should be made visible to readers.

04

OPEN TO REVISION

Being willing to revise a conclusion is part of responsible historical inquiry.

07 • THE METHOD

TRACE
THE CLAIM.

CLAIM ORIGINAL SOURCE CONTEXT CROSS-CHECK SCHOLARSHIP CONCLUSION

This framework is intended to help readers move from a historical assertion to the evidence and reasoning behind it.

08 • OUR COMMITMENT

FOLLOW
THE EVIDENCE.

Our goal is not to manufacture certainty. It is to make the historical record easier to examine, question and understand.
